# Chapter II — Finding and Validating the Idea
*The 10x Method*, pages 26-51

## What this chapter covers

Two big questions: (1) what's a startup idea and how do you find one, and (2) how do you validate it before building. Includes an interview with Mathilde Collin (Front, p.46) and a spotlight on Hexa's own portfolio idea process, plus a section on Hexa ideas that *didn't* pass validation (p.44).

## Key framework: the 3 categories of startup ideas (p.32-35)

| Category | Problem recognition | Existing solutions? | Solution type | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cat 1 — Problem-solution fit | Clearly recognized | No good solutions | New/better way to solve a known pain | Superhuman, Figma, first online tax filing |
| Cat 2 — Market/tech shift | Clearly recognized | Yes, but outdated | New capabilities that didn't exist before | Tandem, Tesla, Notion, ChatGPT for support |
| Cat 3 — Category creation | Not widely recognized | No clear solutions | Entirely new way of doing things | Facebook, Airbnb, Uber, Slack |

Each category has its own sourcing tactics (personal frustration / industry expertise for Cat 1; tracking tech and regulatory shifts for Cat 2; studying behavior and cultural shifts for Cat 3 — the hardest to find but highest potential reward).

## Key framework: how to validate (p.40-43)

Three steps: **become an expert** (talk to users, read everything, find the sharpest pain points) → **scope the idea** (narrow to one wedge, one sentence, explicit non-goals) → **create wireframes** (deliberately rough, using Hexa's own Blocks.pm Figma plugin, to get concrete reactions). Bounded by a validation timeline — a deadline for a go/no-go decision, not open-ended research.
