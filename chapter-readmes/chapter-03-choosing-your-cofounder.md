# Chapter III — Choosing Your Co-founder (and Making It Work)
*The 10x Method*, pages 52-71

## What this chapter covers

Why founders shouldn't go solo (two is the sweet spot), how to find a cofounder, how to assess if they're the right one (the hardest and most important part of the chapter), and how to make the relationship work long-term (roles, equity, rituals). Closes with an interview with Paul Vidal, Cofounder of Collective (p.69).

## Key framework: the 3 ingredients of a great cofounder team (p.56-57)

1. Complementary skills and personalities
2. Alignment on the 5 non-negotiable values
3. Transparency, trust, and communication

## Key framework: the 5 non-negotiables of value alignment (p.61-63)

Long-term vision · Ambition · Commitment · Ways of working · Culture — each with a full question bank (see the `cofounder-alignment-assessor` skill's reference file for the verbatim list).

## Key process safeguards: don't rush (p.64)

- Have at least 3 conversations, including one informal one
- Work together for a week (a short POC)
- Get a second opinion from someone in the same domain
- Run references — at least 2 former managers + 2 former reports, both ways

## How to make it work once committed (p.65-68)

Define roles clearly, split equity deliberately, bring in a cofounder coach early, set up rituals, and realign on values — not just once.

## Notable quotes

> "Value alignment is crucial, especially for making it through the long haul and the tough moments. In our case, it showed clearly: the values we shared as cofounders naturally became the company's values: having a long-term mindset, excellence, and being human." — **Nicolas Saison, CPO and Cofounder of Swan**

> "I think the key step is to have an extremely open and honest conversation right from the start to align expectations: What is everyone's ambition? Will we still be aligned three years from now? If someone wants to leave, what happens?... In my view, this is one of the healthiest practices you can adopt when starting a company." — **Rodolphe Ardant, Cofounder of Spendesk**

**Insight from Amaury Sepulchre, Partner and Cofounder of Hexa** (p.63): "The best way to build trust early is to go first" — talk beyond the business, get personal, be upfront about your own weaknesses first.
