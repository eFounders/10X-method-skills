# Chapter IV — Building the MVP
*The 10x Method*, pages 72-109

## What this chapter covers

How to scope an MVP correctly, how to plan without over-planning (milestones over roadmaps), how to run product rituals, and how to validate early product-market fit via design partners. Closes with 4 founder interviews: Olivier Pailhes (Aircall, p.91), Christophe Pasquier (Slite, p.97), Jordane Giuly (Spendesk, p.102), Nicolas Saison (Swan, p.106).

## Key framework: the MVP building checklist (p.80, verbatim)

1. Aligns with your long-term vision
2. Can be developed in weeks, not months
3. Is a real product and not a polished prototype
4. Delivers enough value to onboard real users
5. Is minimal enough to allow for quick iteration

> "If your MVP plan doesn't check all these, you're overbuilding."

## Key framework: milestones, not roadmaps (p.81-82)

A simple 4-column table (Objective / Why it's a priority / Key things to build / When), reviewed quarterly, never planning more than a quarter ahead. Includes a full worked example from Hexa's internal "LegalX" project.

> "Forget perfect plans, momentum is your greatest asset. In startups, progress comes from shipping fast, not mapping everything out. Because for every 10 features you launch, only one might stick." — **Seraphie de Tracy, CEO and Cofounder of Cohort**

## Product rituals (p.82-83)

Weekly product committees: 30-60 min, everyone working on the product, review what shipped, refine next week's priority, address blockers.

## How to validate early PMF (p.84-88)

Design partners over pilots; focus on one metric post-launch; don't overthink pivoting.
