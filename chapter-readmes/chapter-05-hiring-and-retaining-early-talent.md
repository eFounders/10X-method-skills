# Chapter V — Hiring and Retaining Early Talent
*The 10x Method*, pages 110-139

## What this chapter covers

Who to hire first (engineers, the "founder associate," not a PM too early), how to hire (branding, JD, interview process, scorecards, case studies, references), and how to retain/part ways (salaries, stock options, onboarding, red flags).

## Key framework: the job description, 7 sections (p.120-123)

Title → About the company → What you'll do (missions) → Technical stack → Who you are (requirements) → Why join → Hiring timeline. Full writing rules in the `job-description-builder` skill's reference file.

## Key framework: the scorecard (p.123-125)

Criteria capped at 12, split into Must/Should/Nice to Have, covering hard skills, soft skills, and behaviors, each with a key interview question. Full worked example (Founding Engineer role) in the `scorecard-builder` skill's reference file.

## Other notable frameworks in this chapter

- **Build a brand early-on** (p.120): basic Notion career page, regular LinkedIn posts, selective event participation.
- **The first interview is about convincing, not evaluating** (p.122-123): flip the script at the end of an outbound call — "Do you want to be part of this process?"
- **The importance of follow-ups** (p.123): quick check-ins between interview stages, even a WhatsApp thread.
- **Case study design** (p.125): base it on a problem the founder has already tackled themselves.
- **Reference checks** (p.127): non-skippable; 2 managers + 2 reports, both ways.

## Notable quotes

> "Even when I wasn't actively hiring, I made a habit of adding people on LinkedIn who looked like strong future candidates... When I eventually started recruiting for a PM role, many candidates already knew about Tengo from my posts." — **Hugues Renou, CEO and Cofounder of Tengo**

> "I started posting on LinkedIn mainly for recruitment purposes. Finding great people, engineers especially, who share your mindset is very hard... It's a kind of natural selection: people either connect with your energy or they don't, and that's exactly what you want in a startup team." — **Paul Vidal, CTO and Cofounder of Collective**

> "The goal of the first conversation is not to grill the candidate with technical questions. It's to make them leave thinking, 'I really want to work with these people.'" — **Paul Vidal, CTO and Cofounder of Collective**
