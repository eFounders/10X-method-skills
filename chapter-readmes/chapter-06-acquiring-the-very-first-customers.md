# Chapter VI — Acquiring the Very First Customers
*The 10x Method*, pages 140-179

## What this chapter covers

The founder's role in GTM, defining/refining the ICP, running a Design Partner (DP) program to go from 1 to 10 clients, scaling from 10 to 100 clients, and setting up GTM rituals. Closes with 3 founder interviews: Jonathan Anguelov (Aircall, p.165), Alex Louisy (Upflow, p.170), Rodolphe Ardant (Spendesk, p.175).

## Key framework: the Design Partner program (p.147-151)

- DPs should **pay** — a fixed fee for early access, often split 50% upfront / 50% at program end, with a discount on conversion to long-term customer.
- First call structure: 15 min discovery + 15 min DP deck.
- Qualify with **BANT** (Budget, Authority, Need, Timing).
- Every call ends with a clear status: Not a fit / Not now / Qualified (send an offer) / Second call needed.
- **The DP deck**, 6 sections: value proposition → market pain points → feature overview (or wireframes) → team → DP program details → next steps.
- Program length: ~3 months; weekly check-in + monthly deep-dive; shared Slack channel.
- When it ends: offer a discount/price lock, and (with consent) turn DPs into case studies/testimonials.

## From 10 to 100 clients (p.152-160)

Founder-led GTM continues; leverage DPs with a lookalike strategy; "not-so-cold" outbound; set up a CRM; content beyond the product; understand where the audience hangs out; the power of mini-products; the myth of PLG; customers without borders.

## GTM rituals (p.162-163)

Quarterly GTM rituals + weekly GTM sprints — same cadence philosophy as the product rituals in Chapter IV.

## Notable quotes

> "Have the 'Willingness to Pay' conversation as early as possible — ideally during the first user interviews. If they respond vaguely or seem unsure, that's a red flag." — **Jordane Giuly, Cofounder of Spendesk**

> "People only truly value what they pay for. If they don't pay, they won't use your product with the same intensity, and they won't give you as relevant feedback." — **Vasco Alexandre, CEO & Cofounder of Dotfile**

> "We monetized about two years in. 95% of people said they were willing to pay. But that also meant we clearly waited too long... it was a mistake not to be more firm: either you pay, or you don't use it." — **Christophe Pasquier, CEO & Cofounder of Slite**

> "Setting your price is a craft, not a formula. Mathilde Collin, cofounder of Front, famously asked early design partners to put a price on the value it created for them: 'It won't be free, so tell us what it's worth to you per user.'" — **Quentin Nickmans, Partner and Cofounder of Hexa**

> "What really matters in a Design Partner Program isn't how many partners you have, but how deeply they're committed... Charging can help filter out those who aren't truly motivated, but it also risks excluding high-conviction partners who bring immense value." — **Christophe Barre, CEO & Cofounder of Tandem**
