# Chapter VII — Brand & Communications
*The 10x Method*, pages 180-201

## What this chapter covers

Why brand matters even pre-revenue, how to define your brand (driving force, vision, mission, audience, benefit, differentiator), how to find your "brand feeling" and turn it into a voice, bringing brand to life visually and through communications, and channel-specific guidance (website, founder branding, PR).

## Key framing (p.183)

Brand isn't a late-stage concern — you already have one (landing page, pitch deck, job posts, LinkedIn bio), whether intentional or not. Shaped by two things: **visual identity** (how you look) and **communications** (how you sound). In B2B, purchases are still human — brand wins when functional differences are slim.

## Key framework: brand foundations, 6 questions (p.184)

Driving force · Long-term vision · Mission · Who you serve · Core benefit · What else makes you special — with worked examples from Notion, Stripe, Figma, Superhuman, Slack, Linear.

## Key framework: brand feeling → voice (p.185-187)

Define the emotional response you want (e.g. Spendesk = liberation, Swan = quiet empowerment, Figma = creative freedom), then derive 2-3 voice adjectives from it.

## Channel spotlights (p.192-200)

- **Website**: start with your tagline, follow a recommended structure, keep tips in mind.
- **Founder branding**: align with brand feeling but stay authentic; tell your story.
- **PR**: be genuinely helpful, get journalists interested, be clear on your angle, pitch yourself, make journalists' lives easier, know when to get help.
