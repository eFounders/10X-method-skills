# Brand Foundations Template — The 10x Method

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VII — "Brand & Communications", page 184.

## Why this exists

Consistency is the foundation of every strong brand, but you can't be consistent if you don't know exactly what you're being consistent about. The fix: document your core brand elements in one place — not a fancy brand guide, just a forced clarity exercise about who you are. Refer back to it for everything from landing page copy to strategic decisions.

## The template (verbatim from the book, with its own examples)

| Question | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What's your driving force? | The "why" behind your company — the bigger purpose beyond product or profit. | Notion's driving force is to empower people to unlock their creativity and turn their ideas into reality. |
| What's your long-term vision? | Where you see your company in 5-10 years and the kind of product or world you want to create. | Stripe's vision is to increase the GDP of the internet by making it radically easier to start, run, and scale an online business anywhere in the world. |
| What's your mission? | A short one-liner describing what your product does today and how it helps users. | Figma's mission is to make design accessible to all, by building tools in the browser and enabling collaborative workflows. |
| Who do you serve? | The specific audience or customer segment you're targeting, including their needs, problems, and aspirations. | Superhuman serves busy professionals who spend 3+ hours in their inbox daily and are willing to pay for a premium experience to save time and reduce anxiety. |
| What is your core benefit? | The main value your product provides to users. | Slack makes work simpler, more connected, and more productive. |
| What else makes you special? | Unique qualities, values, or approaches that make your brand stand out. | Linear stands out for their obsessive attention to speed and keyboard shortcuts — reflecting their belief that professional tools should feel as responsive as the professionals using them. |

## Guiding principle

At this stage, brand isn't about a polished, expensive deliverable or a 50-page brand guideline no one reads. It's a focused, intentional effort to signal who you are and why someone should care — "just good enough" to create an emotional hook and help you stand out. You already have a brand whether you're intentional about it or not (your landing page, pitch deck, job posts, LinkedIn bio) — the goal is to make it deliberate.
