# Brand Voice Finder

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VII — "Brand & Communications", pages 185-187.

## Context (per the book)

Your core brand elements (see `brand-foundations-builder`) are crucial for internal alignment, but your audience will never read your vision statement — they'll experience your brand as a *feeling*. Brand feeling is the emotional response you want people to have when they interact with your company: not what your product does, but how it makes people feel.

### Examples from the book

- **Spendesk** taps into a feeling of *liberation* — freeing finance leaders from being "spreadsheet people" and elevating them to strategic business partners.
- **Swan** evokes *quiet empowerment* — helping businesses offer financial services without surrendering their own brand identity ("it's about you, not us").
- **Figma**'s feeling of *creative freedom* translates into a voice that's encouraging, approachable, and collaborative.

You won't get this perfect on the first try, and that's fine — for early-stage startups, brand feeling is a starting point for consistency, refined as you learn more about customers.

## The method (verbatim from the book)

Once you've defined the feeling you want to create, choose **2-3 adjectives** that describe how your brand should speak to evoke it. Each adjective should support the emotional experience: e.g. for "creative freedom," *encouraging* language helps people feel more capable, *approachable* removes friction. You don't need full brand guidelines — just a few clear voice adjectives is enough to shape tone across landing page copy, social posts, job descriptions, even customer support replies.

## Workflow

1. **Get the brand feeling** — if the founder already ran `brand-foundations-builder`, use that output; otherwise ask directly: "when someone interacts with your company, what do you want them to *feel*?" Push past functional answers ("efficient", "fast") toward emotional ones ("relieved", "in control", "quietly powerful").
2. **Propose 2-3 candidate feelings** if the founder is stuck, grounded in their brand foundations (who they serve, their core benefit) — with a one-line rationale for each, similar to the Spendesk/Swan examples.
3. **Once a feeling is chosen, derive 2-3 voice adjectives** that would produce that feeling in writing. Explain briefly what each adjective means in practice (not just the label — how it should show up in a sentence).
4. **Give a short "before/after" example**: take one piece of the founder's actual copy (or write a sample sentence, e.g. a landing page headline or job post opener) and show it once in a generic voice and once in the new defined voice, to make the difference concrete.

## Output format

1. **Brand feeling** (one phrase, with rationale)
2. **2-3 voice adjectives**, each with a one-line explanation of what it means in practice
3. **Before/after example** showing the voice applied to a real sentence
