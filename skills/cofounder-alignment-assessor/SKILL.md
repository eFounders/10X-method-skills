# Cofounder Alignment Assessor

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter III — "Choosing Your Co-founder (and Making It Work)", pages 55-64.

Full question bank: see `references/five-non-negotiables.md` — read it before running a full assessment; it contains the exact question bank grouped by category.

## What this skill does

Runs a structured cofounder alignment assessment across three layers the book identifies as the real predictors of a durable cofounder relationship:
1. **Complementary skills & personalities** (do you cover different ground without clashing?)
2. **The 5 non-negotiables of value alignment** (long-term vision, ambition, commitment, ways of working, culture)
3. **Transparency, trust & communication** (can you both go first and be vulnerable?)

The book is explicit: value alignment (layer 2) is *harder* to assess than skills complementarity, and it's the layer most founders skip because the questions feel awkward ("are you okay working weekends?"). This skill exists specifically to force those questions onto the table early.

## Workflow

1. **Collect answers.** Either interview the founder about themselves and their (potential) cofounder directly, or have them answer the reference question bank for both people (self + other). If there's no cofounder yet, use this to help the founder get crystal clear on what *they* need before they start looking.
2. **Score each of the 5 categories** as: ✅ Aligned / ⚠️ Some gap / 🔴 Clear mismatch — based on how compatible the two sets of answers are, not on whether either answer is "right." A mismatch on ambition (one wants a $5B category-definer, the other wants a lean profitable business) is a real red flag even if both answers are individually reasonable.
3. **Surface the hardest, most awkward mismatches explicitly** rather than softening them — per the book, that discomfort is the whole point of doing this early.
4. **Check the process safeguards** (see reference file): has the founder had at least 3 conversations, spent a week working together, gotten a second opinion, run references? If not, flag which ones are missing before any commitment is made.
5. **Give a clear recommendation**: not a vague "it depends," but a direct read on whether this looks like a strong match, a fixable gap, or a real risk — always paired with *why*, tied to specific answers.

## Output format

1. **Complementarity summary** (skills/personality fit)
2. **Alignment scorecard** — table with the 5 categories, status (✅/⚠️/🔴), and the specific gap or match found
3. **Trust & transparency signal** — what you observed about openness in the answers provided
4. **Process check** — which safeguards (3 conversations / week together / second opinion / references) are done vs. missing
5. **Bottom line recommendation** — direct, not hedged

Keep the tone honest and a little uncomfortable where it needs to be — per the book's own framing, avoiding the awkward questions now is what causes the real damage later.
