# The 5 Non-Negotiables of Cofounder Value Alignment

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter III — "Choosing Your Co-founder", pages 61-63.

Full question bank, verbatim from the book, organized by category. Use this as the assessment instrument — ask both cofounders (or the founder and a candidate cofounder) to answer independently, then compare.

## Self-assessment questions (ask yourself first, before assessing anyone else)
- What energizes you?
- What kind of dynamic drains you?
- Who brought out your best work and why?
- What kind of people or situations created friction?

## Assessing complementary skills and personalities
- What are you great at that you genuinely enjoy doing?
- What do you not want to be responsible for?
- If we started tomorrow, what part of the business would you naturally take ownership of?
- What's one thing you've done in past roles that you'd want to avoid doing again?
- How do you recharge when things get intense?

## Category 1 — Long-term vision
- What kind of company are we trying to build — a category-definer or a niche tool?
- Where do you want this company to be in 5 years? 10 years?
- Are we creating something to disrupt an entire industry, or something lean and profitable?
- Do we both see this venture as our life's work, or a stepping stone?

## Category 2 — Ambition
- Would you sell this for $5M? $100M? $1B?
- Why do you want to be a cofounder instead of joining a startup as an early employee?
- What does "success" look like to you — financially, personally, mission-wise?
- Are we building to flip in 3 years, or aiming for IPO scale?
- How do you want to split equity, and why?
- What's the minimum salary you need to survive? What would "making it" feel like?

## Category 3 — Commitment
- What kind of working pace do you expect?
- Are you okay with weekend work if needed?
- How do you handle pressure or burnout?
- What's your relationship with time — punctual, structured, go-with-the-flow?

## Category 4 — Ways of working
- Where do you want to live and work?
- Are we building in-person, hybrid, or fully remote?
- Are you open to relocating if it becomes necessary?
- Do you have family or other commitments that could affect flexibility or relocation?

## Category 5 — Culture
- Have you managed diverse teams? What's your management style?
- What values do you want to instill in a team?
- What would your ideal culture look like?
- If you had to choose 3 words to describe the culture you want to create, what would they be?

## Assessing transparency, trust & communication
This isn't a question bank — it's a signal to watch for during the whole process: how naturally does openness flow between you? The book's advice (from Amaury Sepulchre, Partner & Cofounder of Hexa): be the one to go first.
- Talk beyond the business — get personal (aspirations, fears, dreams: "What drives you beyond the startup?", "What scares you about this journey?", "Are there personal constraints — finances, family, health — that might shape how you show up?")
- Be upfront about your own weaknesses first; it invites the other person to open up too.

## Process safeguards (don't rush)
- Have at least 3 conversations before deciding — ideally including one informal one (a walk, a coffee, a dinner).
- Work together for a week (a short POC) — the output matters less than how ideas evolve, how you handle ambiguity, whether it feels like progress.
- Get a second opinion from someone in the same domain (a friendly CTO to review a technical profile, a CEO/business lead to skim notes on a business profile).
- Run references: speak to at least 2 former managers and 2 former reports — and offer to do the same for them.
