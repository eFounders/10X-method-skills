# Cofounder Self-Assessment

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter III — "Choosing Your Co-founder", page 61.

## Why this comes first (per the book)

"You have to start by looking inward. Before you can evaluate someone else, you need to understand your own strengths and gaps." Most founders jump straight to assessing candidates without doing this step, which makes it hard to know what complementarity actually looks like for them specifically.

## The self-assessment questions (verbatim from the book)

- What energizes you?
- What kind of dynamic drains you?
- Who brought out your best work, and why?
- What kind of people or situations created friction?

## Workflow

1. **Ask the founder these 4 questions directly**, and push for specific, concrete answers — not "I like working with smart people" but an actual memory or pattern ("I did my best work with X because they pushed back on my ideas instead of agreeing with everything").
2. **Synthesize the answers into a working profile**: what kind of environment, working style, and complementary personality this founder seems to need in a cofounder, based on their own patterns.
3. **Flag the practical implication**: translate the self-assessment into what to actively look for (and actively avoid) in a cofounder search — e.g. if friction came from micromanagement, they should watch for controlling tendencies in candidates; if their best work came from being challenged, they should be wary of someone who agrees with everything.
4. **Offer the natural next step**: once this is done, pair with `cofounder-alignment-assessor` to actually evaluate a specific candidate against this self-knowledge plus the 5 non-negotiables.

## Output format

1. The 4 answers, restated clearly
2. A short "working profile" synthesis (3-4 sentences)
3. "What to look for" / "what to watch out for" — 2-3 concrete traits each, derived directly from the self-assessment
