# Data Room Builder

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VIII — "Fundraising", page 212.

## Context (per the book)

Investors typically use a data room in two stages: (1) before a term sheet, for a quick spot check to validate the story internally, and (2) after a term sheet, as part of due diligence for finalizing the investment.

A good data room is rare at seed stage — having one makes a strong impression, shows organization and seriousness, and makes the investor's job easier. The recommended approach: centralize everything in a single Notion doc so you can share one link and keep it continuously up to date. The structure below is a guide, adapt based on what makes sense for the business.

## The 5-section structure (verbatim from the book)

| Section | Content to include |
|---|---|
| 1. Executive Summary | A concise summary following the 7-question executive summary structure (see `executive-summary-builder`). |
| 2. Market | Discovery interview summary; market size; competition mapping; what makes us different. |
| 3. Product | Product strategy; product demo video; roadmap; product metrics. |
| 4. GTM | GTM strategy; customer testimonials & use cases; revenue model; commercial traction & key metrics; business plan. |
| 5. Team | Team; hiring plan. |

## Common data room mistakes to check for (verbatim from the book, page 213)

- **Treating it as static** — it should evolve; update regularly with new customers, milestones, and quarterly revenue.
- **Assuming investors understand the market** — don't skip the basics; educate before diving into deep specifics.
- **Unclear market sizing** — always show the math behind market size numbers, don't just state a figure.
- **Neglecting long-term vision** — founders over-focus on what's built today, but investors are buying into the future; the data room should tell the story of where the company is going.
- **Overly technical roadmap** — don't list every feature and sprint; highlight outcomes and the big objectives the roadmap unlocks, not implementation detail.
- **Tracking too many KPIs** — more isn't better; curate a concise set of metrics that actually reflect momentum for this specific business model.

## Workflow

1. **Gather what already exists**: does the founder have an executive summary, market research, product metrics, GTM traction data, and team bios already documented anywhere (Notion, decks, spreadsheets)?
2. **Build the data room as a single structured outline**, following the 5 sections exactly, listing what goes in each — pointing to existing source documents where they exist, and flagging clearly what's missing and needs to be created.
3. **Recommend the single-doc approach** explicitly (e.g. one Notion page with the 5 sections as top-level headers, or sub-pages) rather than scattered files across tools — this is the book's explicit recommendation for staying current and shareable via one link.
4. **Prioritize gaps by fundraising stage**: if a term sheet hasn't been discussed yet, the exec summary and market sections matter most for the "spot check" phase; product and team depth become more critical closer to due diligence.
5. **Run the 6 common-mistakes check** against the draft: is the market sizing math shown? Is the roadmap outcome-focused rather than a feature list? Is the KPI set curated rather than exhaustive? Flag any that apply.

## Output format

An outline with the 5 sections as headers, each listing the specific documents/data points that belong there, marked as ✅ Ready / 🔲 To create, based on what the founder has confirmed exists — followed by a quick check against the 6 common mistakes.
