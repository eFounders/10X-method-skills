# Design Partner Deck Builder

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VI — "Acquiring the Very First Customers", page 150.

## Context (per the book)

At this stage, you don't need a polished website or lead-qualification flow. What matters is direct conversations (outreach or warm intros). You won't have a finished product — what you're really selling is your vision and your approach. This deck should be simple and clear, not a 40-slide investor deck.

## The 6-section structure (verbatim from the book)

1. **Your value proposition** — one sentence, crystal clear.
2. **Market pain points** — three key problems you're solving.
3. **Feature overview** — the MVP that addresses the pain points. If the product isn't ready yet, show wireframes instead.
4. **Your team** — showcase your expertise and credibility in the target industry, highlighting how you deliver value beyond just the product at this early stage.
5. **Design Partner Program** — how it works, what they get, and what you expect from them.
6. **Next steps + contact info.**

## Related context to weave in if relevant

- **The DP program is a real sales process.** Structure the first call as: 15 minutes discovery (understand the prospect's need) + 15 minutes on this deck. Qualify with **BANT** (see the `bant-qualifier` skill for the full framework).
- **DPs should pay** — a fixed fee for early access over a defined period, often split 50% upfront / 50% at the end of the program once value is proven, with a discount offered if they convert to a long-term customer afterward. If a founder can't find 5 companies willing to pay even a modest fee, that's a signal worth raising directly.
- **Urgency matters when sending the offer**: always include a validity date.

## Workflow

1. **Gather inputs**: one-sentence value prop, the 3 sharpest pain points the founder has heard from discovery, current MVP status (built vs. wireframes only), founder/team credibility angle, and the terms of the DP program (duration, price, what DPs get).
2. **Write each of the 6 sections** following the book's guidance above — keep language simple and direct, this is a working document for a conversation, not a polished sales asset.
3. **If the DP program terms aren't defined yet**, propose a default structure based on the book's guidance (3-month program, fixed fee split 50/50, discount on conversion) and flag it clearly as a suggested default to validate with the founder.
4. **Close with next steps** that are concrete and time-bound (e.g. "sign by [date] to lock in early-access pricing").

## Output format

A slide-by-slide deck outline (6 sections, each with a suggested headline and 2-4 bullet points of content), ready to be turned into an actual deck or a one-page document. Note explicitly where wireframes should be swapped in if the MVP isn't built yet.
