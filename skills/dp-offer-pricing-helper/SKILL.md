# DP Offer & Pricing Helper

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VI — "Acquiring the Very First Customers", pages 148-149.

## Core rules (verbatim guidance from the book)

- **DPs should pay — always.** A fixed fee for early access over a defined period. "If you can't find five companies ready to pay for your product, even in its earliest form, you probably shouldn't be building it."
- **The fee doesn't need to be high, but high enough to be a real commitment** — find the number that makes the DP care, calibrated to who they are (e.g. $1,000 means nothing to a bank, but real buy-in for a startup or scaleup).
- **Split the payment**: 50% upfront when the product is delivered, 50% at the end of the program once value has been proven.
- **Discount on conversion**: at the end of the DP period, DPs who convert to long-term customers get a discount as thanks for their early commitment.
- **Have the "willingness to pay" conversation as early as possible** — ideally during the first user interviews. A vague or unsure response to a pricing conversation is a red flag about the DP's real level of interest.
- **Motivation matters more than the fee itself** — charging filters out the unmotivated, but some of the most valuable DPs can't afford to pay and are still worth including if they're deeply aligned with the vision (per Christophe Barre's guidance in the book) — assess motivation, not just willingness to pay, especially when learning matters more than cash at this stage.

## Workflow

1. **Get context**: who are the target DPs (company size/type), what's the product's likely future price point, and how many DPs the founder is trying to sign.
2. **Propose a fee** calibrated to the DP's scale — big enough to signal real commitment relative to their size, not an arbitrary number. Explain the reasoning, don't just output a number.
3. **Structure the payment split** (50% upfront / 50% at program end) and the program length (the book recommends ~3 months elsewhere in this chapter — see `gtm-ritual-setup` for the surrounding rituals).
4. **Define the conversion incentive** — what discount or price lock DPs get if they become long-term customers.
5. **Flag the willingness-to-pay signal explicitly**: if the founder describes a prospect as vague or hesitant about paying anything, name that as a red flag rather than something to work around.
6. **Note the exception**: if a prospect can't pay but shows exceptional alignment/motivation, flag that as a judgment call worth making deliberately, not a default to fall back on.

## Output format

1. Recommended fee + rationale
2. Payment structure (split, timing)
3. Conversion incentive
4. A flag on the willingness-to-pay signal if the founder described any hesitation from the prospect
