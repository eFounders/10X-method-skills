# The Early-Stage ESG Checklist — The 10x Method

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter IX — "ESG for Early Stage", pages 232-235.

## Context (per the book)

This checklist is tailored specifically for very early-stage companies — not yet measuring carbon footprint (that typically comes at Series A+), hosting culture celebrations, or running formal board routines with independent directors. It's meant to lay the groundwork now so the company is well-prepared for what comes next. ESG doesn't need to be the top priority in every decision, just like cost isn't always the sole driver — but like financial considerations, it should always be part of the equation.

## E — Environment

Focuses on minimizing ecological footprint and integrating sustainability into all aspects of the business.

**Low-impact operations:**
- Encourage remote-first or hybrid models (often reduces commuting).
- Minimize printing.
- Avoid unnecessary swag or wasteful packaging.

**Carbon footprint awareness (not perfection):**
- No full audit needed at this stage, but be mindful of where emissions come from (travel, shipping, office energy use) to spot quick wins.

**Sustainable procurement:**
- For everything from laptops to office snacks, choose vendors who align with your values (ethical sourcing, recyclable packaging, carbon-neutral delivery).
- Opt for energy-efficient data centers and cloud services running on renewable energy.

## S — Social

Focuses on how the startup engages with employees, customers, and communities, fostering inclusivity, well-being, and social responsibility. (Following the Hiring chapter's guidance sets you up well here.)

**Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI):**
- Build an inclusive and equitable company culture.
- Keep job descriptions concise and run them through free bias-detection tools (e.g. Gender Decoder).
- Standardize the interview process with scorecards for consistency and fairness (see `scorecard-builder`).
- Avoid bias by hiring from diverse networks and ensuring a broad candidate pool for each role.
- Use a salary grid to ensure pay equity.
- Implement an Employee Stock Ownership Plan (ESOP) to promote ownership and fairness.

**Employee well-being:**
- Create a supportive and safe work environment.
- Offer flexibility with work hours and remote work options.
- Schedule regular feedback sessions with employees to improve engagement and development.
- Communicate the company's mission and purpose regularly to create a sense of belonging.

## G — Governance

Focuses on transparency, ethical decision-making, and strong management practices.

**Founding team alignment = early governance:**
- If you have cofounders, treat the working relationship like a board: regular check-ins, document big decisions, agree on escalation paths for conflicts.
- Create a basic decision-making framework (who leads on what, how tie-breakers work) — prevents bigger issues later.
- Maintain clear reporting lines and transparency; document key decisions, financials, and milestones so investors, advisors, and employees are well-informed.

**Prepare for future governance:**
- Establishing rhythms of clear communication, documentation, and accountability now pays off when raising money or building a board later.
- Aim for diversity and independence as the advisory/board structure grows — outside perspectives reduce blind spots and build credibility.

**Data ethics:**
- Create guidelines for ethical data and AI usage. If dealing with AI or large datasets, ensure clear guidelines exist for ethical, non-biased, transparent use. Address potential ethical issues early to avoid misuse.
- Think early about SOC 2 and compliance (e.g. GDPR) — doesn't need to be priority #1 today, but awareness matters.
- Implement strong data protection measures: strong password practices, two-factor authentication, secure communication/storage tools. Train the team (even a quick onboarding doc) on spotting phishing, managing credentials, and handling sensitive data.
