# Executive Summary Builder

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VIII — "Fundraising", pages 208-210.

Full template with detailed guidance per section: see `references/exec-summary-template.md` — read it before writing, and follow its structure exactly.

## Why this comes first (per the book)

Do not write the pitch deck before nailing this narrative — working in slides too early leads to weak framing. Get the story to ~95% clarity here first. Treat this as a living document you rehearse out loud, refine continuously, and only then turn into slides (see `pitch-deck-structure-builder` for that next step).

## The 7 questions

1. What opportunity are you addressing
2. Why are you the perfect team to address this opportunity
3. What's your step one, and why is it smart
4. The path to $100M ARR
5. Why now?
6. Why will you win?
7. What do you want the investor to remember

## Workflow

1. **Gather inputs** from the founder: the problem they're solving and for whom, their personal/team connection to the problem, their current traction or first concrete step, their long-term scale vision, what's changed recently that makes now the right time, and their structural edge (speed, distribution, data, team, brand...).
2. **Write each section as 2-4 tight sentences** — this is a summary, not a deck; avoid vague market-size stats per the book's explicit warning ("avoid vague market stats").
3. **Make the "why now" and "why will you win" sections concrete**, not generic — tie "why now" to a real market/technology/regulatory shift if possible, and "why will you win" to a specific structural advantage, not just "we'll execute better."
4. **End with one crisp "key takeaway" line** — the single sentence the founder wants an investor repeating to their partners after the meeting. This should echo through the rest of the summary, not be disconnected from it.
5. **Flag if any section feels weak or generic** — per the book, this is exactly where founders under-invest, and it's worth being direct about it rather than polishing weak content.

## Output format

A markdown table with 2 columns (Section | Content), one row per of the 7 questions, followed by a short note on which sections feel strongest/weakest and need more work before moving to a full deck.
