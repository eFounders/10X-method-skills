# Executive Summary Template — The 10x Method

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VIII — "Fundraising", pages 208-210.

## Context (per the book)

Start crafting your story from the very beginning of your company and keep refining it continuously as you build. A compelling narrative doesn't appear overnight. Start with a simple executive summary — treat it as a living document, iterate as the company evolves, and only build it into a full pitch deck once the narrative reaches ~95% clarity. Do not write the actual pitch deck before the narrative is nailed — working in slides too early leads to weak framing.

Use the executive summary to rehearse the pitch: in front of a mirror, with teammates, with trusted advisors, or a coach.

## The 7-question structure (verbatim from the book)

| Section | Description |
|---|---|
| What opportunity are you addressing | Define the problem space. What is broken, inefficient, or underserved? Why does this matter now, and who feels the pain most acutely? Avoid vague market stats. |
| Why are you the perfect team to address this opportunity | Investors bet on teams before products at this stage. Why you? What unique insight, expertise, or experience do you bring? If this is a space you've lived in or struggled with, say so. |
| What's your step one and why is it smart | Every big vision needs a practical starting point. What's the first concrete step you're taking to make the long-term vision real? Step One should feel ambitious but achievable. |
| The path to $100M ARR | Your masterplan: the vision for turning an idea into a $100M ARR business and the steps to get there. Proves two things: (1) you're solving a real, pressing problem, and (2) your idea can scale massively. |
| Why now? | What's changed in the world that makes this the right moment? Show you understand the landscape and why the timing wasn't right 5 years ago but is now. Good opportunity to talk about traction. |
| Why will you win? | Every founder thinks they'll win — your job is to make it believable. What structural advantage are you building: speed, distribution, product, data, team, brand? How will you out-execute others in the same space? |
| What do you want the investor to remember | This is where you shape perception. If an investor has to summarize your pitch in one line after the meeting, what do you want them to say? Repeat your key message multiple times. |

## Pitching prep notes worth passing on

- Front-load all fundraising prep (story, exec summary, deck, data room, choice of lawyers) before the roadshow starts — once pitching begins, there's no time to fix the deck.
- Practice on 4-5 funds you know aren't the right fit first — live practice, real feedback, sharper pitch before the "dream" VCs.
- Anticipate objections in advance (competitor funding news, crowded market perception) with data-backed responses; some belong in the data room, others should be rehearsed live.
