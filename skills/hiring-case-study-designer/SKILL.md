# Hiring Case Study Designer

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter V — "Hiring and Retaining Early Talent", page 125.

## The core rule (per the book)

Founders often ask: "How do I evaluate someone in a role I don't fully understand?" The book's answer: ideally, you've done the job yourself first (a CTO who built the first product version, a CEO who's already closed the first sales) — that hands-on experience gives you the context to know what good looks like.

**The case study you assign should be based on something you've already tackled yourself.** This lets you compare the candidate's thinking and output against your own real experience.

**Critical constraint**: a case study is never about getting free work or outsourcing a question you're curious about. Its purpose is to evaluate how someone approaches real, relevant problems — not to teach the founder something new. If the exercise would produce output the founder could actually use for the business, that's a signal it's the wrong exercise (or needs to be reframed/paid).

## Workflow

1. **Ask the founder for a real problem they've personally solved** in this role or function — not a hypothetical, an actual decision or piece of work they did themselves early on.
2. **Turn it into a bounded exercise** the candidate can complete in a defined, reasonable amount of time (be explicit about the time-box — respecting candidate time matters, especially early-stage where candidates are also evaluating the company).
3. **Define what you're actually evaluating** — not "did they get the right answer" (there often isn't one objectively right answer) but the *process*: how they broke down the problem, what tradeoffs they considered, how they communicated their reasoning.
4. **Sanity-check the "free work" trap** explicitly: would the candidate's output be directly usable by the business? If yes, either scope it down to something illustrative but not directly reusable, or plan to pay for the work.
5. **Pair with the scorecard** (see `scorecard-builder`) — the case study should map back to specific criteria on the scorecard, not be evaluated in isolation.

## Output format

1. The case study prompt itself, time-boxed, written for the candidate
2. What you're actually evaluating (2-4 dimensions, tied to scorecard criteria if available)
3. A flag confirming this isn't disguised free work — or a note on how to adjust it if it is
