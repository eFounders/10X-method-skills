# ICP Definer & Refiner

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VI — "Acquiring the Very First Customers", pages 143-146.

## Core framing (per the book)

A great ICP is narrow and precisely scoped, but getting there **starts broad, not narrow**. It's normal — even good — to define 2-3 candidate ICPs early on rather than forcing one right answer immediately. The goal at this stage is to generate strong candidates to test, not to land on "the one" prematurely.

## The ICP attributes to consider (verbatim from the book)

- **Company size** — be specific, not generic ("100-300 employees" not "SMB"), tied to when a real workflow break or role (e.g. an IT director) appears.
- **Sector** — which verticals get the most value (healthcare, fintech, e-commerce...)?
- **Geo** — regional requirements matter (e.g. a US finance product might need QuickBooks integration, an EU one needs Cegid + GDPR alignment).
- **Role** — be crystal clear on who feels the pain *and* who holds the purchasing decision; anchor the ICP to whoever actually signs, not just whoever benefits daily.
- **Team size** — sometimes the relevant unit isn't company size but the size/composition of the specific team being sold to (e.g. finance teams with 4+ people).
- **Tools used** — if the product fits next to or replaces part of an existing tool (Salesforce, HubSpot, QuickBooks), that's a real signal — especially if most best-fit users already use that tool.
- **Willingness to buy** — beyond who they are, define what makes them *ready*: pain now, authority to decide, budget to move.

> "Many founders aren't ruthless enough when defining their ICP. It should feel almost absurdly narrow." — Vasco Alexandre, CEO & Cofounder of Dotfile

## The staged approach (per the book)

1. **Discovery stage** — deliberately broad, for fast learning. Hugues Renou (Tengo) revisited and refined the ICP every 15 days based on new conversations.
2. **Testing with design partners** — see who gets the most value (and who doesn't); the DP program (see `design-partner-deck-builder`) exists partly to test ICP hypotheses directly.
3. **3 key signals to define an ICP** (per Hugues Renou): who you can engage in meaningful conversation, who you can convert, and who actually *sticks* with the product. Short contracts (e.g. monthly instead of annual) can deliberately surface this third signal faster — higher short-term churn, but the churn itself reveals who isn't really ICP.
4. **Ongoing refinement** — the ICP stays a living hypothesis even after DPs; keep testing through outbound, inbound, and sales conversations. Spendesk, for example, discovered small companies (3-person teams sharing one card) weren't their ICP — the real pain only appeared once a company had a finance manager/CFO role holding the card.

## Feedback loop between ICP and MVP (p.146)

When something doesn't click, the instinct is to tweak the ICP toward a "better-fit" customer — but sometimes the real insight is about changing what's being *offered*, not who's being targeted. As new segments emerge using the product differently, it can be worth shifting the ICP toward them and evolving the product to serve them better — this becomes a flywheel: a more valuable product attracts better-fit customers, which further sharpens product direction.

## Workflow

1. **Get the current ICP** (even rough/guessed), and the founder's current stage (pre-DP discovery, mid-DP testing, or post-DP scaling).
2. **Build 2-3 ICP hypotheses** using the attribute list above if none exist yet — don't force a single answer prematurely.
3. **If DPs or early customers already exist**, apply the 3-signal test (engage / convert / stick) to see which hypothesis is actually holding up.
4. **Check for the flywheel signal**: is there a segment responding unexpectedly well that the product wasn't originally built for? If so, flag it as a possible ICP shift opportunity rather than noise to ignore.
5. **Sharpen toward "absurdly narrow"** once there's real signal — resist the urge to keep the ICP broad for reach; narrow ICPs make both product and sales conversations easier.

## Output format

1. Current or hypothesized ICP(s) — 1-3 candidates, described using the attribute list (company size, sector, geo, role, team size, tools used, willingness to buy)
2. Which stage the founder is at (discovery / testing / refining) and what evidence supports or challenges each hypothesis
3. A sharpened, more specific ICP recommendation if enough signal exists — or the next concrete test to run if not
