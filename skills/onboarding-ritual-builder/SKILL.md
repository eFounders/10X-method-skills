# Onboarding Ritual Builder

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter V — "Hiring and Retaining Early Talent", page 135. Section: "Onboarding."

## Framing (per the book's broader themes)

The book repeatedly emphasizes that consistency and routine — not one-off improvisation — are what make early-stage processes work (see the same philosophy applied to product rituals in `product-rituals-setup` and GTM rituals in `gtm-ritual-setup`). Onboarding is one of the highest-leverage moments to get this right: it's a new hire's first real signal about how the company actually operates, right after the JD and interview process sold them on joining (see `job-description-builder`).

## Workflow

1. **Get the role and context**: what's the role, who's onboarding them (which founder/team member), and what does success look like in their first 30-60-90 days.
2. **Build a structured first-week plan**, not just a list of admin tasks — cover: what they need to know about the company/product on day one, who they need to meet and why, what their first real piece of work should be (ideally something they can ship or contribute to quickly, per the book's broader "execute relentlessly" and "weeks not months" philosophy from earlier chapters), and how/when they'll get their first feedback.
3. **Make it repeatable, not bespoke** — the point of a "ritual" is that the second and fifth hire get the same quality of onboarding as the first, without the founder reinventing it each time. Write it so a future team member could run it without the founder personally walking through it.
4. **Tie it to the "what success looks like in 6 months" framing** from the job description (see `job-description-builder`) if that was already defined for the role — onboarding should set up the trajectory promised in the JD, not just cover logistics.
5. **Build in an early feedback checkpoint** — a short, scheduled check-in (e.g. end of week 1, end of month 1) rather than leaving it ambiguous when the new hire will first hear how they're doing.

## Output format

A simple structured plan: Day 1 → Week 1 → First 30 days, each with 3-5 concrete items (people to meet, first task, what "on track" looks like at that checkpoint), plus one explicit feedback checkpoint date.
