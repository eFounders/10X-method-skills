# Pitch Deck Structure Template — The 10x Method

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VIII — "Fundraising", pages 210-211.

## Context

There's no one-size-fits-all deck — every company's story is different. This is a recommended flow that works well as a starting point, to be tailored to the founder's actual narrative (which should already be nailed via the executive summary — see `executive-summary-builder`).

## The 11-section structure (verbatim from the book)

| Section | Description |
|---|---|
| Vision & ambition | Start with the big picture. What future are you building, and why is it exciting? Your chance to inspire. |
| Pain / opportunity | What fundamental problem exists today, and why is it worth solving? Frame it clearly and show why it matters now. |
| Proposed solution / offer | What are you building, and what makes your approach uniquely suited to solving this problem? Highlight differentiation. |
| Founder–market fit | Why are you the right team to build this? What gives you an edge — insight, experience, obsession? |
| Market size & landscape | Show the opportunity is large, but be precise. Don't just drop a $1T number — define your target segment, explain how it grows, position relative to existing players. |
| Product overview | Clear view of what you've built (or are building). Screenshots, demos, architecture diagrams help. Keep it high-level but tangible. |
| Pricing & business model (if relevant) | How do you make money? What's the underlying logic of your revenue model? If too early, share working hypotheses. |
| Traction & proof points | Show what you've achieved so far — key OKRs, usage metrics, paying customers, retention, live use cases. |
| Team | Who's building the company today, and who are the key early hires? Highlight functional excellence and entrepreneurial drive. |
| Strategic roadmap & fundraising rationale | Where are you headed over the next 12-18 months, and how does this round help you get there? Be clear on use of funds and milestones. |
| Path to $100M+ ARR | Describe how this becomes a generational company. What makes that outcome credible? What does the company look like at scale? |

## 6 tips from Amaury Sepulchre, Partner & Cofounder of Hexa (verbatim guidance)

1. **Tailor your story** — this list isn't exhaustive. Prioritize what makes your story strong; remove anything that doesn't serve that goal.
2. **Hypothesis-driven funding** — every round should test a clear set of hypotheses. State what you're trying to prove or disprove, and the metrics you'll use to measure progress.
3. **No exit slides** — don't include an "exit" slide at this stage. If an investor asks for one, they're not aligned with your long-term ambition.
4. **TAM discipline** — avoid vague or inflated TAMs. Be specific about your entry point and show how you expand over time. A clear go-to-market beats a trillion-dollar pie chart.
5. **Include a "Reasons not to invest" slide** — key beliefs investors must align with to invest, representing potential challenges/risks they need to understand and accept.
6. **Path to $100M+ ARR** — one of the hardest parts to tell, but essential: articulate a credible path to a billion-dollar business. What would it actually take? Why is that future plausible based on what you know today? Investors want to dream with you, but need a credible path shown.
