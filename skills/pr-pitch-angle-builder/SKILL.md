# PR Pitch Angle Builder

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VII — "Brand & Communications", pages 197-200.

## Be genuinely helpful, before you need anything (p.197)

Identify journalists who regularly cover the industry, read their work, understand their angles. If there are gaps in their reporting, offer feedback, data, or expert connections — build the relationship *before* pitching. The best press relationships are built on mutual value, not transactional asks.

## Is this actually newsworthy? (p.197)

Journalists won't cover a first sales hire, and landing a customer alone rarely cuts it unless it's a major, high-profile name. What can work: a significant product launch, a noteworthy funding round, or traction signaling a broader trend — but even then, it needs sharp positioning to stand out. Journalists write for their audience, not to advertise a startup — tailor the pitch to what that audience actually cares about.

## The 4 filters to cut through the noise (p.198, verbatim)

- **Uniqueness** — what's genuinely new or different? Why is this startup uniquely positioned to solve it better than anyone else?
- **Relevance** — how does the story tie to a broader industry shift or cultural trend? Frame it beyond "what the product does" into a growing problem or unmet need.
- **Humility** — avoid buzzwords and vague claims, especially amid AI-jargon saturation; journalists spot fluff instantly.
- **Expertise** — demonstrate real command of the industry, not just the product, to become a go-to source for future stories too.

**Example from the book**: Roundtable didn't just announce a €3M seed round — they framed it as *"100 business angels from across Europe back a new way to fund startups"*, aligning with their mission and tapping into a live discussion about early-stage investing. That sharper angle secured tier-one coverage.

## Be clear on your angle (p.198-199)

Imagine choosing the headline of tomorrow's coverage — that's the level of clarity needed. The ideal headline should guide the press release and the pitch, appear in the subject line and opening paragraph, and be echoed throughout. If the positioning is unclear, the framing is left entirely to the journalist — and it may miss the mark.

## Pitch yourself (p.199, verbatim structure)

Before Series A, a PR agency is rarely worth the cost — pitch directly:
1. **A short, authentic pitch email**: lead with the news and why it matters, explain the product in 1-2 sentences, link the press release, zoom out to why this matters right now.
2. **A thoughtful target list**: look at who covered competitors, read their recent work, prioritize outlets the ideal audience actually reads (including vertical/industry press).
3. **Personalize every email**: reference something specific the journalist wrote, keep it brief and buzzword-free, offer real value (interviews, data, insights).

"Sending 5-10 thoughtful emails is infinitely more effective than blasting dozens of generic pitches."

## Make journalists' lives easier (p.199-200, verbatim)

- Prepare a press release (a Notion doc is fine): neutral/factual/third-person, opinions expressed only through 2-4 concise quotes max, lead with who/what/when/where/why, one page, strong clear headline.
- Include a media kit: founder bios, product screenshots, logo files, key facts/figures, customer testimonials.
- Be available and responsive; don't micromanage the story once published — only correct factual errors.
- For high-priority announcements, consider an embargo with a clear publication date — signals professionalism.

## When to get help (p.200)

A full PR agency is rarely worth it before Series A, but a freelance PR consultant can help when: there's no idea where to start / no time / no media contacts for a first big announcement; there's a sudden surge of inbound journalist interest to prioritize; there are live interview opportunities (TV, radio, panels) needing media training; or an international announcement needs localization. Even with help, the founder should stay actively involved in messaging — never fully outsource relationship-building.

## Workflow

1. **Get the news/milestone** and check newsworthiness honestly against the book's bar (launch, funding, or trend-signaling traction — not routine hires or small deals).
2. **Run it through the 4 filters** (uniqueness, relevance, humility, expertise) and sharpen to one headline-level angle.
3. **Build the target list** (competitor coverage → journalists → outlets the real audience reads).
4. **Draft the short pitch email** (news + why it matters + 1-2 sentence product explainer + zoom-out) and 2-4 press-release-ready quotes.
5. **Flag DIY vs. "get help"** based on the book's specific trigger situations.

## Output format

1. The sharpened one-line angle (audience-first, headline-style)
2. A short pitch email draft
3. A starter target list approach (how to build it, not necessarily named journalists)
4. 2-4 ready quotes + key facts for a one-page press release
5. A DIY vs. get-help recommendation
