# Round Structuring Helper

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VIII — "Fundraising", pages 213-215.

## What instrument? (p.213-214)

At Seed, most founders need capital without wanting to set a formal valuation yet — **SAFEs** (Simple Agreements for Future Equity) or **BSA AIR** (the French equivalent) are the go-to instruments. Why: fast and lightweight (minimal legal work, a fraction of the cost of a priced round), and flexible (can raise progressively, closing small tickets over time rather than needing a full investor cohort upfront).

A typical SAFE has 3 core terms: **the amount invested, a valuation cap, and a discount on the next round's valuation**. No new shares are created at signing — the price per share doesn't change, and SAFE holders aren't yet formal shareholders (they don't appear on the cap table until conversion). This simplicity can create discomfort for some investors (they're in limbo, not quite equity holders), and for founders the real challenge is **dilution forecasting** — since conversion happens later at the next priced round, it's hard to know exactly how much ownership will be given away. Use with care when stacking multiple SAFEs/BSA AIRs with different terms — misaligned terms can snowball into complexity.

## Get your cap table in order (p.214, verbatim action points)

The cap table is the single source of truth on ownership — it must be clean, accurate, and ready *before* fundraising starts (not scrambled together once a term sheet lands). Action points:
- Have a precise, accurate pro-forma cap table in Excel format
- Get lawyer assistance, but founders should stay in control of their own cap table
- After the seed round, use a reputable cap table management tool (Carta, Pulley, or Equify in France)
- Reflect all equity issuances — SAFEs, convertible notes, options, warrants — with correct terms and timestamps
- Ensure founder vesting schedules are properly documented

Investors review the cap table meticulously for dilution, rights, and ownership stake — ambiguities or inconsistencies here are a common, avoidable red flag.

## Choosing the right counsel (p.215, verbatim guidance)

Lock in a lawyer and agreed fees **before** fundraising starts, and especially before a term sheet lands — VCs use time pressure as a tactic (reputable funds have issued term sheets late on a Friday with a 3-day expiration window). Scrambling for counsel mid-deal is the worst position to be in.

**How to choose**: ask founders in your network who recently raised a similar round; pick someone locally recognized as a VC expert where your investors are based; look for experience across Series A/B/C (more balanced positions, better long-term planning — the best lawyers know cash is tight early and may offer flexible terms betting on the long-term relationship); ask for client references and verify they've advised companies through *successive* rounds, not just at seed.

**Working with counsel**: be transparent about goals and timeline; remember their job is to defend your interests; always ask about "market practice" during negotiations (you can deviate for specific situations, but knowing the norm keeps your positions credible); have them prepare a due diligence checklist and virtual data room, and do a "pre-diligence" review of documents before going to market; have them walk you through every document before signing.

## Workflow

1. **Get the founder's stage and round size** — instrument choice and norms vary by stage/geography.
2. **Walk through the SAFE/BSA AIR vs. priced equity tradeoff** using the book's framing (speed/simplicity/flexibility vs. dilution forecasting difficulty and investor discomfort with limbo status) — surface the right questions for their lawyer, don't replace legal advice.
3. **Run the cap table checklist**: pro-forma cap table in Excel? All instruments reflected with correct terms/timestamps? Vesting documented? Tool in place post-seed?
4. **Run the counsel checklist**: lawyer and fees locked in before fundraising starts? Experience across multiple rounds? References checked?
5. **Explicitly flag this is directional prep, not legal advice** — the founder should have this conversation with an actual lawyer, informed by these questions.

## Output format

1. Instrument considerations tailored to stage/round size (SAFE/BSA AIR vs. priced equity tradeoffs)
2. Cap table hygiene checklist (✅/🔲 against the 5 action points)
3. Counsel checklist (locked in early? multi-round experience? references verified?)
4. Explicit note: this is fundraising prep, not a substitute for actual legal counsel
