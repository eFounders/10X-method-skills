# Scorecard Template & Worked Example — The 10x Method

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter V — "Hiring and Retaining Early Talent", pages 123-125.

## The rules (verbatim guidance from the book)

A scorecard should:
- Categorize skills into **must-have, should-have, and nice-to-have**.
- Include both **hard skills** (technical abilities) and **soft skills** (communication, problem-solving).
- **Limit criteria to a maximum of 12** — more than that makes the process too complex.
- Link each mission/responsibility in the role to a corresponding question — always ask the same structured questions to every candidate to eliminate random judgment and make comparisons reliable.

A good scorecard also makes rejection easier: instead of debating feelings, you have objective reasons to fall back on.

## Worked example — Founding Engineer scorecard (verbatim from the book)

| Criteria | Priority | Skill Type | Expectations | Key question to ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum 5 years of experience, preferably in a startup | Must Have | Hard Skill | We expect someone who has held at least two different positions, with a cumulative experience of 5+ years. Startup experience is a plus, but the person must at least be aligned with the startup mindset. | Can you walk me through your career path and how your responsibilities evolved over time? |
| Proficient in TypeScript – front & back (min 3 years), expert in ReactJS and NodeJS | Must Have | Hard Skill | The role is full-stack; the candidate must have hands-on experience with both front-end and back-end (React and NodeJS). Mastery of TypeScript in a production environment is essential. | Can you describe a project where you used React, Node.js, and TypeScript together in production? |
| Infrastructure / cloud experience (AWS/GCP, Docker, microservices) | Should Have | Hard Skill | While not mandatory, it's appreciated if the candidate can manage the end-to-end development of a feature, including deployment and infrastructure-related tasks. | Can you share an experience where you managed the deployment and infrastructure of a feature, using tools like AWS or Docker? |
| Experience or interest in AI | Nice to Have | Hard Skill | The candidate should be interested in working within the AI space. Prior experience in Generative AI is a plus, but a solid understanding of key concepts is important. | If you had to integrate an AI feature into a product tomorrow, where would you start? |
| Project management | Must Have | Hard Skill | Should have successfully managed a complex, scalable project involving multiple stakeholders, tight timelines, or changing contexts. | What's your process for breaking down a large, ambiguous task into actionable steps? |
| Problem-solving ability | Must Have | Soft Skill | The candidate should be able to handle entire problems, thinking through solutions and implementing them effectively. Strong problem-solving skills are essential: understands the problem, prepares, finds and supports solutions, communicates clearly. | Walk me through a time when your first solution to a problem didn't work. What did you do next? |
| Gets things done | Must Have | Soft Skill | A doer with a strong bias toward action. | Have you ever gone out of scope to get something important done? |
| Team player | Must Have | Soft Skill | Must prioritize team success, be collaborative, and contribute to peer learning (e.g. peer programming, mentoring, coaching). | Tell me about a time when you helped a teammate grow? |
| Entrepreneurial mindset | Must Have | Behavior | Thrives in ambiguity and ownership. We want proactive individuals who take initiative, go the extra mile, and are autonomous in their work. | If you were to start your own company, what would it be and why? |

## Case study guidance

Founders often ask: "How do I evaluate someone in a role I don't fully understand?" The book's answer: ideally, you've done the job yourself first (built the first product version as a technical founder, or closed the first sales as a CEO) — that hands-on experience is what lets you judge what "good" looks like.

The case study you assign should be based on something **you've already tackled yourself** — this lets you compare the candidate's thinking and output against your own experience. Important: a case study should never be a way to get free work or outsource a question you're curious about — its purpose is to evaluate how someone approaches real, relevant problems, not to teach you something new.
