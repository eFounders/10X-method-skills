# Website Structure & Tagline Builder

Source: *The 10x Method* (Hexa), Chapter VII — "Brand & Communications", pages 191-193.

## Context: pick the message before the channel (p.191-192)

A common mistake is starting with the channel (deciding to launch a podcast/blog, then scrambling for something to say) — backwards. Define the core message first (see `brand-foundations-builder`, `brand-voice-finder`), then choose the channel best suited to carry it. Early on, one or two channels is enough — the website is almost always one of them, since it's often the first (sometimes only) place users, candidates, or investors encounter the company.

## The tagline (p.192, verbatim rules)

The tagline is usually the headline on the landing page — the first thing people see. It should clearly express the core benefit (from the brand foundations doc) while reflecting the brand feeling (from `brand-voice-finder`). A good early-stage tagline is:
- **Clear** — no jargon or clever wordplay that hides the point
- **Benefit-led** — what the user gets, not what was built
- **Emotionally toned** — calm, bold, empowering, urgent... whatever fits the brand feeling
- **Short** — aim for 5-8 words

## The page structure (p.193, verbatim)

- **Hero**: tagline, 1-2 supporting lines, and a clear call to action
- **Social proof**: logos, testimonials, or early traction to build credibility
- **Benefits**: key benefits/use cases (e.g. "prototype quickly")
- **Key features**: 3-4 features with light visual support (icons, screenshots)
- **How it works**: a simple 3-step overview of the user journey
- **Call to action**: repeat the primary CTA
- **FAQ**: pre-empt questions, remove friction to conversion

## Tips to keep in mind (p.193, verbatim)

- Don't try to say everything — focus on what converts best
- Lead with outcomes — the headline should tell people what they'll gain
- Write for skimmers — clear headers, bullets, short paragraphs
- Prioritize benefits over features — talk about them, not you
- Stay aligned with the brand feeling — what emotion should this provoke?
- Keep it human — simple, confident language
- Be ruthless — only include what helps someone say yes

## Workflow

1. **Pull in brand foundations and voice** if available (`brand-foundations-builder`, `brand-voice-finder`) — the tagline and copy should be a direct application, not written in isolation.
2. **Draft the tagline first**, separately from the rest of the page. Offer 3-5 options at 5-8 words each, each clearly benefit-led and matched to the brand's emotional tone — check each against the 4 criteria (clear / benefit-led / emotional tone / short).
3. **Build the page structure** using the 7 sections above, with suggested copy for each — grounded in the founder's actual product/benefits, not generic placeholder text.
4. **Apply the "tips" as a final filter**: cut anything that doesn't help someone say yes; check the copy reads well skimmed (headers, bullets); confirm the tone matches the brand feeling throughout, not just in the hero.

## Output format

1. 3-5 tagline options (5-8 words each), each with a one-line rationale against the 4 criteria
2. A full 7-section page outline with suggested copy for each section
